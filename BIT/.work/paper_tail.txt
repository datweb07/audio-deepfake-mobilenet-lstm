P095	[heading1]	Conclusion
P096	[heading2]	Practical and Societal Implications
P097	[p1a]	LAVA can support transparent artifact selection for defensive voice-authentication and media-verification screening, while retaining human review for consequential decisions. Its offline diagnostic evidence is not a guarantee of field security, demographic fairness, or edge-device readiness.
P098	[heading2]	Limitations
P099	[Normal]	Evidence is limited by heterogeneous training and calibration, a fixed 100-recording diagnostic subset, simulated rather than physical replay, and one desktop CPU. The dataset cannot establish speaker, source, generator, parent-recording, or cross-dataset independence. No unseen-corpus, edge-device, streaming, or multi-seed evaluation was conducted; RSS is whole-process memory, EfficientNet-B0 is an available warm-up checkpoint, and paired scores for sample-level prediction-flip analysis are unavailable.
P100	[heading2]	Concluding Remarks
P101	[Normal]	Recommendations remain artifact-, objective-, and platform-specific. Under the evaluated objectives, ShuffleNetV2 is the stronger predictive-performance candidate: it achieved the highest clean AUC, lowest clean EER, highest mean stressed F1, and remained non-dominated at RTF 0.0208. MobileNetV3 is the lower-latency candidate on the measured host. These findings do not identify a universally optimal model or rank architectures independently of their available artifacts.
P102	[Normal]	LAVA compares six heterogeneous voice anti-spoofing artifacts under traceable contracts. MobileNetV3 and ShuffleNetV2 are non-dominated under the evaluated objectives, while low-SNR noise is the main weakness on the fixed diagnostic subset. Future work should add paired full-stress scores, multi-seed training, unseen corpora, physical replay, causal streaming, and representative edge hardware.
