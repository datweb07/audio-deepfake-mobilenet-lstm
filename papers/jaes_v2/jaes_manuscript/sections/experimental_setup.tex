\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Cohorts and conditions}

For the resource-bounded medium cohort, there are 640 ASVspoof 2019 LA training utterances, evenly divided between the REAL and FAKE categories and with attack IDs A01-A06. Development data set A contains 112 utterances each of the REAL and FAKE categories, using seven speakers. Development data set B includes 144 REAL recordings, using nine speakers. The registered audit will use disjoint training and development speaker IDs. The same development data sets are used by the three independently initialized blocks, hence the blocks will measure sensitivity to training data and not a fresh test set.

Seven scored views for each model include a clean, two gain controlled, Noise5, Opus64, and the two reversed chains. The stress endpoint is made of Noise5 and chains. In chains, there is a constant-absolute 10 dB noise configuration and 24 kb/s Opus encoder in each order. Noise5 and Opus64 are distinct conditions. The corresponding standalone Noise10 and Opus24 were done on seeds 9107 and 9207. Seed 9307 will give only development order scores. Table~\ref{tab:design} gives an overview of fixed design.

The medium sample was selected because it would allow equal CPU runs for both REAL and FAKE classes. It includes 320 REAL and 320 FAKE samples. The full training partition contains 2,580 REAL and 22,800 FAKE recordings, so a comparison with that partition would change both training size and class mixture.

\subsection{Paired uncertainty and efficiency}

For each seed, differences are channel minus ERM. Percentile intervals use 10,000 paired speaker-cluster bootstrap draws with seed 91109. A and B are sampled separately. Within a sampled speaker, all sources and their views retain their paired model scores. This preserves source and speaker dependence and avoids treating transformed views as independent trials. Mean, sample standard deviation and range across the three blocks are descriptive. They do not estimate a population confidence interval over random initializations.

All the eight fits were performed on a Windows CPU platform. The optimizer time and validation time are drawn from the run log and may include iterations after interruptions. The registered evaluation method maintains the 0.5 cut-off point without the calibration step.

\subsection{Nested training-size comparison}\label{sec:size-design}

This larger group of training has the original 640 samples and another 640 samples of the training set. This dataset has 640 REAL and 640 FAKE samples for 20 speakers and covers A01-A06. The attack counts are 106, 108, 108, 108, 106 and 104. The audit evaluated audio hash uniqueness and disjoint IDs, speaker IDs and audio hashes compared to the development, calibration (reserved), and locked out subsets. Lineage of utterances is still not complete. The two bigger limbs share the same fresh initialization that their smaller 640 peers have at seed 9207. All four models employ the same 368 development utterances, seven conditions, clean BCE checkpoint choice and threshold 0.5.

For endpoint M, the size difference will be the difference between the two size augmentation differences:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:size-contrast}
\begin{split}
\Delta_s &= M_{\mathrm{Channel},s}-M_{\mathrm{ERM},s},\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{size}} &= \Delta_{1280}-\Delta_{640}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}

Higher positive differences mean higher ROC-AUC ranking and higher error rate for false alarm and miss. Both cohort sizes have 21 epochs, meaning that cohort size doubling also results in doubling the budget of optimization steps from 840 to 1,680. The estimand is sensitivity under this fixed-epoch policy. We conducted 10,000 pairs of bootstrap draws for speakers per each development stratification, using the same weight values for all four models fitted and all perspectives from the samples of speakers. Pointwise intervals capture the evaluation cohort variation given the fitted models. Training-seed and training-cohort variability are outside these intervals.

\subsection{Design controls and support}

Development A and B serve different purposes. A provides both classes needed for ROC-AUC, EER and fixed threshold risks. B introduces an additional stratum of REAL-only speakers that helps to measure false alarm sensitivity. Merging B into A would alter the inherent weighting of speakers as well as the set of REAL-FAKE pairs used. The two strata are kept separate. Class counts in the mixed-class stratum are provided by 112 unique recordings per class. The three stress views add up to 336 observations per class but still rely on those same recordings.

For each seed block, the two fitted models get the identical set of development sources and transformed waveforms. Between seeds, different initializations produce different fitted functions, while the evaluation cohort is identical. Thereby, we have two types of pairing. Source pairing determines the input to a comparison of the methods, while seed blocking is responsible for different initializations and source ordering in fitting. All three seed blocks use the same speaker population. All attacks A01-A06 occur in training. Hence, the attack rows represent variation in attack IDs across stress.

Order experiment places the same noise realization either before or after the codec with the same nominal clean-referenced signal-to-noise ratio. However, the achieved characteristics after compression might differ. Assuming the equality of achieved SNR or bandwidth would eliminate some part of the intervention. The controls allow comparing those particular pipeline combinations and not the simulations of packet loss, device reverberation, proprietary enhancement or communications stack altogether.

\subsection{Interpretation of the resampling intervals}

All observations from the sampled speakers will remain clustered \cite{field}. Within each replicate, the speaker weights will be the same for the ERM and the channel within a stratum. If a speaker is sampled multiple times, it will contribute the repeated cluster weights to the calculation of the contrast, which then repeats its calculations based on those weights and reports the percentile limits among the valid draws. Thus, repeated views of the same source and the predictions in the paired arms remain linked through their sampling process. For rank statistics, the constraint on class support will be applied through the resampling procedure. The record of the number of valid draws allows the interval to take into account the draws that are undefined.

The intervals obtained based on seven or nine speakers only provide some empirical support to the percentile interval approach. The large effect from one speaker may alter the confidence interval and the selection of the attacks represented within the resample. The intervals are conditioned on the fitted models and, hence, do not reflect any change of the training seed. Descriptive summary is used to describe the variability between different seeds.

\subsection{Evidence boundaries}

The prospective comparison encompasses the registered score orientation, 0.5 operational rule, budget, clean-loss criterion for selecting check points, and the stress condition. The pair of records determines the unit in which the methods are compared. The additional Noise10 and Opus24 runs for 9107 were introduced to serve as diagnostics on the same fitted models and development sets. This helps in understanding the deviation from the additive output assumption without any independent new-cohort replication.

\subsection{Locked Held-Out Evaluation}\label{sec:heldout-design}

The main holdout set contains 1,088 audios from 67 speakers from the official ASVspoof 2019 LA countermeasure evaluation partition \cite{asv19}. Using a deterministic hash-based ranking and a speaker round-robin with seed 91109 we have 256 REAL and 64 FAKE recordings for each attack A07 to A19. The audit was reconciled with the archive and did not find any matches between the selected subset and training, development, and calibration manifests for shared source ID, speaker ID and audio SHA-256 digest. Completeness of the generator and utterance lineage verification remains to be done.

The lock includes the four development checkpoints from 9107 and 9207 and four conditions per source: clean, Noise5, Noise10 then Opus24, and Opus24 then Noise10. All 17,408 predictions were made. Seeds 9307 and both fits with 1,280 recordings provide only the development evidence. The sigmoid score is oriented towards FAKE and the score larger than 0.5 goes to FAKE. Macro stressed ROC-AUC takes an average of the three stress-condition AUCs. The clean and stressed error rates have class-conditional denominators and the stressed denominator uses three correlated views per source. The held-out contrast holds within-seed source pairing and applies the same speaker-bootstrap to the held-out speakers. Development A and B refer to the early development strata only.

Locked sources were matched against their respective identity locks. All per-source score shards preserve full model and condition sets and have been validated prior to analysis. Registered metric values and associated uncertainties have been recomputed from committed predictions. The selected sources, models, transformations, and threshold are locked before scoring. The attack-balanced partition has distinct support from development partitions A and B; thus, differences in effect magnitude across partitions are only descriptive.

The ordered views employ source decoding to 22.05 kHz and truncation at 3 s followed by the standard peak limit of 0.25. A source-ID-keyed random selection provides a single fixed-absolute Noise10 vector normalized against clean reference power. Both chains use this vector. The codec chain uses FFmpeg with libopus at nominal rate of 24 kb/s, PCM16 input, aligned timestamps and decoding to 22.05 kHz. There is no post-channel normalization of the waveform before P0. The use of the common noise vector does not mean identical post-codec signal-to-noise ratios. Development replay verifies the composed waveforms and score anchors. Held-out evidence uses locked implementation and scores.
