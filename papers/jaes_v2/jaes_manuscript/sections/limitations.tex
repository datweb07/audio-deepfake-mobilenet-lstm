\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}\label{sec:limits}
The 640-source cohort is a compute-bounded training design. It does not represent full-foundation training. A and B have limited speaker support, and attack groups have 18 or 19 sources. The bootstrap describes uncertainty under speaker resampling on these fitted models and this cohort. Related conditions within a source are dependent. Secondary subgroup diagnostics are descriptive and no multiplicity adjustment is implied.

The two initialization blocks share the same development data and checkpoint rule. The same partition supports checkpoint selection and the reported development analysis. The results are sensitive to the fixed decision threshold and selected checkpoint. In particular, guard failure applies to the selected checkpoint comparison. It does not imply that every epoch or threshold would fail.

Only one architecture was retrained in the matched design. Signal preprocessing, noise scaling, and codec response can change the score contrast independently of forensic cue retention. The current experiments provide no verified independent forensic target. The pending official evaluation is within the same corpus release, and some evaluation attacks reuse algorithms or frameworks. Cross-corpus, cross-architecture, and deployment robustness remain open.

\subsection{Internal and external validity}
The signal intervention controls the configured processing and uses matched inputs, but the clean cohort determines which speakers and synthesis examples can contribute. Within release, reuse of attack families prevents a simple unseen-generator interpretation. Further, the detector selects checkpoints on clean development BCE, which can couple the reported clean performance to the selection data. The registered selection prevents retrospective adjustment to the stress effects while leaving this dependence intact. Effects on other operating points and threshold policies would need a separate locked design.

The source is the unit for linking ordered views, and the speaker is the resampling unit. A model sees correlated transformed versions of the same speech signal at evaluation. A larger view count does not replace additional speakers. In particular, attack-level intervals depend on a limited number of source and speaker clusters. A different bootstrap specification, broader cohort, or leave-one-speaker-out analysis could change interval width. The current inference is limited to the reported conditional comparisons.

\subsection{Engineering scope}
Execution integrity, deterministic replay checks and sealed artifacts support reproducibility of the fitted comparisons. They do not establish that a production implementation has the same numerical or latency properties. The measurements use a CPU with the registered environment and include training and validation work. They provide no evidence about mobile latency, energy, streaming, or adversarial adaptation. A bounded within-release evaluation is planned under separate authorization. Even after it is complete, a broader deployment claim will require separate measurements under representative codecs, networks, devices, languages and authentic speech conditions.

\subsection{Construct validity and paired uncertainty}
A FAKE score is a classifier output. It can change when the transformation affects spectral structure, temporal texture, signal scale or the preprocessing input range. Without an independently defined cue and intervention, the change cannot distinguish altered evidence from altered sensitivity to the same evidence. The matched components provide a check against an overly simple additive interpretation, while the one-block retrospective scope limits replication.

The within-seed paired estimates reflect a common test population and depend on the registered speaker clusters. The selected clean checkpoint is another source of uncertainty because different realizations of the training corpus or selection data could select another epoch. The observed differences across two blocks are useful robustness diagnostics but cannot estimate a reliable distribution over seeds, architectures or corpora. The manuscript therefore reports the two contrasts separately and treats their average as descriptive.
