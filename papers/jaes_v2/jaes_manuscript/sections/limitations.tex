\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}\label{sec:limits}
Both nested training cohorts are balanced and compute-bounded. Full-foundation training was outside the design and would change the class mixture from 50\% FAKE to 89.83\% FAKE. The size contrast uses one initialization and one nested expansion, with twice as many updates at 1,280. It leaves fixed-compute size effects and robustness across independent cohort draws unresolved. A and B have limited speaker support, and development attack groups have 18 or 19 sources. The speaker bootstrap keeps the fitted models fixed and preserves dependence among views from the same source.

The three development initialization blocks share the same development data and checkpoint rule. The same partition supports checkpoint selection and the reported development analysis. The results are sensitive to the fixed decision threshold and selected checkpoint. In particular, guard failure applies to the selected checkpoint comparison. It does not imply that every epoch or threshold would fail.

Only one architecture was retrained in the matched design. Signal preprocessing, noise scaling, and codec response can change the score contrast independently of forensic cue retention. The current experiments provide no verified independent forensic target. The completed official evaluation uses four 640-recording models within the same corpus release. Some evaluation attacks reuse algorithms or frameworks. An external corpus with verified audio terms, source lineage and disjoint speakers and utterances is needed to test cross-corpus transfer. Cross-architecture and deployment robustness also remain open.

\subsection{Internal and external validity}
The signal intervention controls the configured processing and uses matched inputs, but the clean cohort determines which speakers and synthesis examples can contribute. Within release, reuse of attack families prevents a simple unseen-generator interpretation. Further, the detector selects checkpoints on clean development BCE, which can couple the reported clean performance to the selection data. The registered selection prevents retrospective adjustment to the stress effects while leaving this dependence intact. Effects on other operating points and threshold policies would need a separate locked design.

The source is the unit for linking ordered views, and the speaker is the resampling unit. A model sees correlated transformed versions of the same speech signal at evaluation. A larger view count does not replace additional speakers. In particular, attack-level intervals depend on a limited number of source and speaker clusters. A different bootstrap specification, broader cohort, or leave-one-speaker-out analysis could change interval width. The current inference is limited to the reported conditional comparisons.

\subsection{Engineering scope}
Execution integrity, deterministic replay checks and sealed artifacts support reproducibility of the fitted comparisons. They do not establish that a production implementation has the same numerical or latency properties. The measurements use a CPU with the registered environment and include training and validation work. They provide no evidence about mobile latency, energy, streaming, or adversarial adaptation. The completed evaluation uses a bounded, attack-balanced within-release subset. Representative codecs, networks, devices, languages, and authentic speech conditions require separate measurements.

\subsection{Construct validity and paired uncertainty}
A FAKE score is a classifier output. It can change when the transformation affects spectral structure, temporal texture, signal scale or the preprocessing input range. Without an independently defined cue and intervention, the change cannot distinguish altered evidence from altered sensitivity to the same evidence. The matched components provide a check against an overly simple additive interpretation, while the one-block retrospective scope limits replication.

The within-seed paired estimates reflect a common test population and depend on the registered speaker clusters. The selected clean checkpoint is another source of uncertainty because different realizations of the training corpus or selection data could select another epoch. The observed differences across three blocks are useful robustness diagnostics but cannot estimate a reliable distribution over seeds, architectures or corpora. The manuscript therefore reports each development contrast separately and treats their mean and sample SD as descriptive. The locked held-out evidence covers the original two pairs.

The held-out sample contains 64 sources per FAKE attack and 256 REAL sources from 67 speakers. This quota design changes attack weighting relative to the complete official partition. The attack intervals are pointwise and do not control multiplicity. Highlighting A17--A19 and decomposing specific errors were post-hoc choices, so subgroup patterns remain exploratory even where individual intervals exclude zero. These recordings were first evaluated with a locked design. Subsequent model selection on their scores would consume that separation. Generalization across corpora, generators, training scales and architectures remains unresolved.
